# Case 02 — Cloud & Infrastructure Product

**Role:** Technical Product Owner  
**Domain:** Cloud / Infrastructure / Platform  
**Environment:** Enterprise Technology  
**Methods:** Agile / Scrum / Kanban  
**Tools:** Jira, Miro

## Context

Cloud and infrastructure initiatives combined physical infrastructure, service implementation, cloud providers, network connectivity, operational requirements and multiple technical dependencies.

The product challenge was to move from individual technical requests toward an integrated view of the product lifecycle: what needed to be delivered, why it mattered, what depended on it and how it would be operated.

## Example initiative

One initiative involved the implementation of cloud capabilities starting from physical infrastructure acquisition and preparation, through service implementation and multicloud integration testing.

The work crossed infrastructure, network, cloud and service teams. Procurement, hardware readiness, datacenter activities, technical dependencies and delivery sequencing all affected the product timeline.

## Product approach

I treated the initiative as a product flow rather than a collection of disconnected technical tasks.

### Discovery

I first clarified:

- The expected business and operational outcome
- The product/service boundaries
- Stakeholders and technical owners
- External and internal dependencies
- Infrastructure constraints
- Security and operational requirements
- Delivery risks and sequencing

### Backlog structure

Complex initiatives were organized into a hierarchy of Epics, Features and Stories / Technical Stories so that the team and stakeholders could see the relationship between the objective and the technical work.

Where a story was too large or a specialist could become blocked by a long-running initiative, the work was decomposed into smaller deliverable units.

### Prioritization

Priorities considered:

- Business and product impact
- Dependencies and critical path
- Operational risk
- Delivery deadlines
- Team capacity
- Governance and production-support constraints

## Cloud context

The environment included GCP, OCI and hybrid/multicloud scenarios. Technical considerations included cloud instances, IAM, storage, networking, NAT, connectivity, cost and availability.

My technical background helped translate these considerations into product decisions and made dependency discussions with engineering teams more effective.

## Delivery governance

Production-impacting changes followed established change-management practices, including defined ownership, risk assessment, approvals, implementation planning and rollback considerations.

As Product Owner, I tracked the impact of changes on scope, priority and delivery commitments rather than treating delivery as isolated technical execution.

## Outcome

The main product outcome was improved visibility and coordination across a technically complex initiative. Stakeholders could understand what was being delivered, why it was prioritized, which dependencies existed and where constraints could affect the timeline.

## Key takeaway

Cloud Product Ownership is not limited to a roadmap or backlog. In infrastructure products, value depends on understanding the complete chain from physical and virtual resources through connectivity, platform services, operations, security, dependencies and lifecycle evolution.
