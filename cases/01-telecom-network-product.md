# Case 01 — Telecom & Network Product

**Role:** Technical Product Owner  
**Domain:** Telecom / Network Infrastructure  
**Environment:** Enterprise Technology  
**Methods:** Scrum, Kanban, Product Discovery  
**Tools:** Jira, Miro

## Context

In a large-scale enterprise technology environment, telecom and network products involved multiple technical teams, operational dependencies and stakeholders. Network architecture, infrastructure, security, observability and service operations were frequently interconnected.

The Product Owner needed to connect technical and business needs, structure initiatives, make priorities visible and coordinate dependencies without losing sight of operational constraints.

## Challenge

A technical request rarely represented a single team's work. A seemingly simple requirement could involve network architecture, security, infrastructure, observability, application dependencies, change management and operational support.

The challenge was to transform this complexity into a product structure that teams could understand, refine and deliver.

## Product approach

I used a structured flow:

1. Understand the problem and expected outcome.
2. Identify stakeholders, users, dependencies and constraints.
3. Map technical impact and operational risk.
4. Break initiatives into **Epics → Features → Stories / Technical Stories**.
5. Refine scope with technical leadership and engineering teams.
6. Prioritize according to business impact, dependencies, operational risk, urgency and capacity.
7. Maintain roadmap and backlog visibility.
8. Track delivery, impediments and changes with stakeholders.

## Technical context

The work required practical understanding of enterprise networking and infrastructure, including LAN/WAN, routing and switching, network architecture, datacenter connectivity, cloud connectivity, firewalls, security, observability and hybrid environments.

My infrastructure background allowed me to challenge assumptions, identify dependencies earlier and communicate directly with engineers and technical specialists.

## Stakeholder management

The product environment included technical leaders, network and telecom specialists, infrastructure teams, security and observability functions, application/solution areas and business-facing stakeholders.

A key responsibility was maintaining a shared view of priorities while recognizing that teams also had production support, governance requirements and other initiatives competing for capacity.

## AI-enabled initiatives

The telecom/network product context also included initiatives involving AI agents and interactions with specialized AI, observability and AIOps teams.

My role was not to replace the specialist teams responsible for AI implementation. The Product Owner responsibility was to understand the product problem, expected behavior, dependencies, constraints and evolution path well enough to lead the initiative and coordinate delivery.

## Key takeaway

A Technical Product Owner does not need to implement every component. The differentiator is understanding the product and its technical context deeply enough to make informed decisions, expose dependencies, challenge assumptions and keep engineering and stakeholder priorities aligned.
